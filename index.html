Boiling Water:
Pressure, Altitude & the Open vs. Closed Container

Why water doesn't always boil at 100°C — and what a pressure cooker, a mountain, and an airplane have to do with it.

Scroll to begin↓
01 · The basics

What is boiling?

Water molecules are never still. They jiggle, bump and bounce off each other all the time.

Temperature is just a measure of how fast they're moving on average. Heat the pot, and they speed up.

Boiling happens when molecules gain enough energy to break free from their neighbours and escape as gas — steam. Bubbles of vapour form inside the water, rise, and burst at the surface.

Rising bubbles = water turning into vapour. Wisps above = steam escaping.

02 · Open vs. closed

Where does the steam go?

Boiling is a tug-of-war: molecular energy pushing out vs. pressure pushing down. Change the pressure, and you change the boiling point.

Steam escapes freely, so pressure stays at atmospheric. At sea level the water boils at 100°C (212°F) — and stays there no matter how high you turn the flame. Extra heat just makes more steam.

Steam can't escape, so pressure builds. Higher pressure pushes back on the molecules, so the boiling point rises — water can reach ~120°C (248°F) before boiling.

That's why pressure cookers cook faster: hotter water transfers heat into food much quicker.

⚠ Danger: a sealed, overheated pot stores huge energy. If it suddenly opens, pressure drops, the superheated water flashes into steam all at once — an explosive release. That's why pressure cookers have safety valves.
03 · Altitude

Climb higher, boil cooler

The air above you has weight. The higher you go, the less air is stacked on top — so atmospheric pressure drops, and water boils at a lower temperature.

04 · Real life

Why it matters

🍝

Pasta & eggs

At altitude, boiling water is cooler, so food cooks slower. Recipes add time — or cooks reach for a pressure cooker.

✈️

Airplanes

Cabins are pressurized to feel like ~6,000–8,000 ft. Coffee on board is brewed with water that boils below 100°C.

🍵

Tea on Everest

Water boiling at ~82°C can't fully extract black tea. Your brew comes out weak, no matter how long it "boils".

🧪

Vacuum chambers

Pump out enough air and water boils at room temperature — bubbling furiously while still cool to the touch.

05 · Takeaway
Boiling isn't about heat alone — it's about the balance between molecular energy and the pressure pushing back.

Fun fact

On Mars, air pressure is so low that liquid water would boil away at around 10°C or less.

Fun fact

Deep-sea hydrothermal vents shoot out water above 400°C — the crushing ocean pressure keeps it from boiling.
